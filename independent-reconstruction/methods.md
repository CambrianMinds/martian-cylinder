## 1. Primary Data Provenance and Radiometric Calibration

All measurements and modeling in this investigation were derived directly from primary NASA Planetary Data System (PDS3) Reduced Data Record (RDR) products generated by Malin Space Science Systems (`MMMRDRGEN pds18.0`, data set `MSL-M-MASTCAM-4-RDR-IMG-V1.0`):

1. **`3556MR1025170721700585C00_DRXX.IMG` / `.LBL` (Primary Radiometric & Geometric Reference)**:
   - **Dimensions**: $1328\text{ samples} \times 1184\text{ lines} \times 3\text{ bands}$ (`BAND_SEQUENTIAL`, 16-bit big-endian unsigned integer `>u2`, 12-bit active dynamic range `0..4095`, `INVALID_CONSTANT = 4096`).
   - **Detector Subframing**: Acquired on Sol 3556 (`2022-08-07T20:58:23.349` UTC, Local True Solar Time `12:10:25`, $L_s = 281.015^\circ$) by Mastcam-100 Right (`MAST_RIGHT`, serial `3004`, filter `MASTCAM_R0_CLEAR` centered at $575\text{ nm}$, exposure $14.2\text{ ms}$). Subframed from the $1648 \times 1200$ CCD starting at 1-based detector coordinates $(\text{FIRST\_LINE\_SAMPLE}, \text{FIRST\_LINE}) = (161, 17)$.
   - **Onboard Compression & Companding**: Downlinked using `INST_CMPRS_MODE = 1` (`"PREDICTIVE LOSSLESS BAYER HUFFMAN ENCODING"`) with hardware companding lookup table `MMM_LUT0`. Because the onboard spatial compression is **lossless** Huffman encoding of the Bayer pattern rather than JPEG Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) compression, **no $8\times 8$ JPEG block artifacts exist in the primary data**. The dominant instrumental effects at the pixel scale are:
     1. *Bayer CFA demosaicing* (`C00` ground interpolation across RGBG subpixels), which introduces local $3\times 3$ to $5\times 5$ spatial covariance and minor inter-band chromatic edge shifts (quantified independently across Red, Green, and Blue bands in `step3_feature_extraction.py`).
     2. *Optical Point Spread Function (PSF)* blur at focus step `2705` (best focus distance $3.957\text{ m}$).
     3. *Companding quantization* from the 8-bit-to-12-bit `MMM_LUT0` inversion.
   - **Linear Spectral Radiance Conversion**: Raw 16-bit DN values are converted to spectral radiance $I_b(x,y)$ in $\text{W m}^{-2}\text{ sr}^{-1}\text{ nm}^{-1}$ via the PDS label parameters (`RADIOMETRIC_CORRECTION_TYPE = MMMRAD1`, `DARK_LEVEL_CORRECTION = 122.9`):
     $$I_b(x, y) = \text{RADIANCE\_OFFSET}_b + \text{RADIANCE\_SCALING\_FACTOR}_b \times \text{DN}_b(x, y)$$
     with $\text{RADIANCE\_OFFSET} = (0, 0, 0)$ and $\text{RADIANCE\_SCALING\_FACTOR} = (1.201\times 10^{-4}, 1.112\times 10^{-4}, 1.037\times 10^{-4})\text{ W m}^{-2}\text{ sr}^{-1}\text{ nm}^{-1}/\text{DN}$ for bands $(R, G, B)$. Mean spectral radiance $I(x,y) = \frac{1}{3}\sum_{b=0}^2 I_b(x,y)$ is used as the primary luminance field, and each individual band is analyzed separately to test for chromatic/demosaicing bias.

2. **`3556MR1025170721700585C00_DRLX.IMG` / `.LBL` (Geometrically Linearized 16-Bit Reference)**:
   - **Dimensions**: $1323\text{ samples} \times 1180\text{ lines} \times 3\text{ bands}$ (`>u2`, 16-bit linear radiance), geometrically warped by MSSS ground software from the raw `CAHVOR` radial-distortion camera model to an ideal pinhole `CAHV` camera model. Cross-correlation around the target region confirms a uniform integer translation $(\Delta x, \Delta y) = (-3, -3)\text{ px}$ ($r = 0.9978$) between `DRXX` and `DRLX`.

3. **`3556MR1025170721700585C00_DRCX.IMG` and `_DRCL.IMG` (8-Bit Color-Stretched Products)**:
   - Used solely to verify that non-linear 8-bit tone mapping (`DRCX`/`DRCL`) does not alter our geometric conclusions. All primary measurements are performed on the unstretched 16-bit linear radiance products (`DRXX` and `DRLX`).

## 2. Camera Geometry (`CAHVOR` and `CAHV`) and Coordinate Transformations

### 2.1 Intrinsic Camera Parameters
For a linear `CAHV` camera model with perspective center $\mathbf{C} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, unit optical axis $\mathbf{A}$, horizontal projection vector $\mathbf{H}$, and vertical projection vector $\mathbf{V}$ (all expressed in `ROVER_NAV_FRAME` in the PDS label):
- Principal point $(h_c, v_c)$ and effective focal scales $(h_s, v_s)$ in pixels/radian are extracted via:
  $$h_c = \mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{H}, \quad v_c = \mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{V}, \quad h_s = \|\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{H}\|, \quad v_s = \|\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{V}\|$$
- Orthonormal image-plane basis vectors $(\hat{\mathbf{h}}', \hat{\mathbf{v}}', \mathbf{A})$ are given by:
  $$\hat{\mathbf{h}}' = \frac{\mathbf{H} - h_c \mathbf{A}}{h_s}, \quad \hat{\mathbf{v}}' = \frac{\mathbf{V} - v_c \mathbf{A}}{v_s}$$

For the full `CAHVOR` model (`DRXX`), a 3D point $\mathbf{P}$ in `ROVER_NAV_FRAME` with relative position $\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{P} - \mathbf{C}$ is projected to 0-based image pixel coordinates $(x, y)$ using Gennery's (1992) radial distortion equations:
$$\omega = \mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{O}, \quad \boldsymbol{\lambda} = \frac{\mathbf{p}}{\omega} - \mathbf{O}, \quad \tau = \boldsymbol{\lambda} \cdot \boldsymbol{\lambda}$$
$$\mu = R_0 + R_1 \tau + R_2 \tau^2, \quad \mathbf{p}_{\text{corr}} = \mathbf{p} + \mu (\mathbf{p} - \omega \mathbf{O})$$
$$x = \frac{\mathbf{p}_{\text{corr}} \cdot \mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{p}_{\text{corr}} \cdot \mathbf{A}}, \quad y = \frac{\mathbf{p}_{\text{corr}} \cdot \mathbf{V}}{\mathbf{p}_{\text{corr}} \cdot \mathbf{A}}$$
Inverse ray casting $(x, y) \mapsto \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\text{ray}}$ is performed by initializing with the exact closed-form `CAHV` ray $\mathbf{r}_0 \propto (\mathbf{V} - y\mathbf{A}) \times (\mathbf{H} - x\mathbf{A})$ ($\mathbf{r}_0 \cdot \mathbf{A} > 0$) and refining via Gauss–Newton iteration on tangent coordinates $(\alpha, \beta)$ until $\|\mathbf{x}_{\text{pred}} - (x,y)\|_2 < 10^{-12}\text{ px}$.

### 2.2 Extrinsic Frame Transformations (`ROVER_NAV_FRAME` $\to$ `SITE_FRAME`)
In MSL PDS conventions, `ROVER_NAV_FRAME` ($+X$ forward, $+Y$ starboard, $+Z$ downward) is related to local level `SITE_FRAME` ($+X$ North, $+Y$ East, $+Z$ nadir/downward) by `ORIGIN_OFFSET_VECTOR` $\mathbf{T}_{\text{rov}}$ and scalar-first quaternion $q = (q_0, q_1, q_2, q_3) = (0.3101999, -0.1235113, -0.0378476, 0.9418538)$:
$$\mathbf{v}_{\text{site}} = \mathbf{R}(q) \, \mathbf{v}_{\text{rov}}, \quad \mathbf{P}_{\text{site}} = \mathbf{T}_{\text{rov}} + \mathbf{R}(q) \, \mathbf{P}_{\text{rov}}$$
Transforming the `DRLX` camera axis $\mathbf{A}$ into `SITE_FRAME` yields azimuth $\text{atan2}(A_y, A_x) = 227.7412^\circ$ and elevation $\arcsin(-A_z) = -22.0047^\circ$, matching `FIXED_INSTRUMENT_AZIMUTH = 227.7412` and `FIXED_INSTRUMENT_ELEVATION = -22.0047` in `DERIVED_IMAGE_PARMS` to all 6 significant figures.

### 2.3 Local Perspective Jacobian and Distortion Anisotropy at Target
At the target centroid $(x_0, y_0) = (1061.0, 980.0)$ in `DRXX`, the viewing ray lies only $2.505^\circ$ off the camera axis $\mathbf{A}$ ($2.391^\circ$ off the optical distortion axis $\mathbf{O}$). Evaluating the $2\times 2$ angular-to-pixel Jacobian $\mathbf{J} = \partial(x,y)/\partial(\theta_h, \theta_v)$ via Singular Value Decomposition yields singular values $\sigma_1 = 13498.24\text{ px/rad}$ and $\sigma_2 = 13418.43\text{ px/rad}$ (geometric mean focal scale $f_{\text{local}} = 13458.28\text{ px/rad}$, instantaneous field of view $\text{IFOV} = 74.304\ \mu\text{rad/px}$). The local optical distortion anisotropy is:
$$\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_2} - 1 = 0.005948 \quad (0.595\%)$$
confirming that camera lens distortion across the object's $\sim 45\text{ px}$ ($\sim 3.3\text{ mrad}$) extent alters apparent aspect ratios by less than $0.6\%$.

## 3. Subpixel Boundary Extraction and Noise Model

### 3.1 Empirical Noise and Blur Characterization
High-frequency radiometric noise $\sigma_I$ is estimated from a homogeneous regolith patch (`x: 1000..1030, y: 925..942`) by subtracting a $\sigma = 1.5\text{ px}$ Gaussian background trend, yielding $\sigma_I = 7.782\times 10^{-3}\text{ W m}^{-2}\text{ sr}^{-1}\text{ nm}^{-1}$. Across the sunlit object boundary ($I_{\text{bright}} = 9.848\times 10^{-2}$, $I_{\text{shadow}} = 1.358\times 10^{-2}\text{ W m}^{-2}\text{ sr}^{-1}\text{ nm}^{-1}$), the contrast ratio is $7.25:1$ and the per-pixel edge signal-to-noise ratio is $\text{SNR}_{\text{edge}} = 10.91$.

### 3.2 Directional Subpixel Profile Extraction and Physical Edge Polarity
To avoid integer-pixel grid discretization bias, boundaries are sampled along orthogonal 1D normal profiles $\mathbf{p}(s) = \mathbf{p}_{\text{base}} + s \hat{\mathbf{n}}$ using bilinear (`order=1`) and bicubic (`order=3`) interpolation:
1. **Physical Edge Polarity (`version = "physical-edge-polarity-v2"`)**:
   Given normal vector $\hat{\mathbf{n}} = (-\hat{t}_y, \hat{t}_x)$ pointing outward toward the exterior:
   - **Upper Exterior Boundary (Dorsal Limb)**: The physical material transition is from the bright sunlit rock body ($I \approx 0.098\text{ W m}^{-2}\text{ sr}^{-1}\text{ nm}^{-1}$) to the darker background regolith ($I \approx 0.045\text{ W m}^{-2}\text{ sr}^{-1}\text{ nm}^{-1}$) along $+\hat{\mathbf{n}}$. This physical drop in brightness corresponds to `grad_sign = +1` in the profile search coordinate. (Algorithms using `grad_sign = -1` inadvertently lock onto internal brightness depressions inside the rock body rather than the true material boundary).
   - **Lower Exterior Boundary (Ventral Margin)**: The transition from the bright body to darker foreground soil along $+\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ corresponds to `grad_sign = -1`.
2. **Parametric Error-Function (`erf`) Edge Model**:
   $$I(s) = I_{\text{low}} + \frac{I_{\text{high}} - I_{\text{low}}}{2}\left[1 + \operatorname{erf}\left(\frac{s - s_0}{\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_{\text{PSF}}}\right)\right]$$
   fitted via non-linear least squares (`scipy.optimize.curve_fit`) within a local window around the directional gradient extremum, yielding subpixel transition coordinate $s_0$, Gaussian blur width $\sigma_{\text{PSF}}$, step contrast $|I_{\text{high}} - I_{\text{low}}|$, and formal covariance uncertainty $\sigma_{s_0}$.
3. **Non-Parametric Parabolic Gradient Peak**:
   The profile is smoothed with a $\sigma = 0.65\text{ px}$ Gaussian kernel, differentiated with respect to $s$, and the directional extremum of $\pm \partial I/\partial s$ is refined to subpixel precision via 3-point parabolic interpolation.
4. **Total Edge Uncertainty**:
   For each extracted boundary point $i$, the orthogonal position uncertainty $\sigma_{\text{eff}, i}$ combines the formal profile fit uncertainty $\sigma_{\text{fit}, i}$, half the difference between the `erf` and parabolic-gradient estimators $\frac{1}{2}|s_{\text{erf}, i} - s_{\text{grad}, i}|$, and a systematic pixelation/demosaicing floor $\sigma_{\text{sys}} = 0.12\text{ px}$ in quadrature:
   $$\sigma_{\text{eff}, i} = \sqrt{\max\left(\sigma_{\text{fit}, i},\, \tfrac{1}{2}|s_{\text{erf}, i} - s_{\text{grad}, i}|,\, 0.05\right)^2 + 0.12^2}$$

## 4. Geometric Fitting and Model Comparison Criteria

### 4.1 Total Least Squares (Orthogonal Distance Regression) for Linear Features
For $N$ points $\{(x_i, y_i)\}$ along a longitudinal boundary, the best-fit line $\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot (\mathbf{x} - \bar{\mathbf{x}}) = 0$ is obtained via eigendecomposition of the $2\times 2$ covariance matrix, minimizing true orthogonal residuals $v_i = \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot (\mathbf{x}_i - \bar{\mathbf{x}})$. Deviations from straightness are quantified by fitting a quadratic polynomial $v(u) = c_0 + c_1 u + c_2 u^2$ along the longitudinal coordinate $u = \hat{\mathbf{t}} \cdot (\mathbf{x}_i - \bar{\mathbf{x}})$, yielding curvature $\kappa \approx 2|c_2|$, maximum sagitta $\delta_{\text{sag}} = |c_2| (L/2)^2$, and curvature significance $z_\kappa = |c_2| / \sigma_{c_2}$. Angle uncertainties are computed via 1000-iteration non-parametric bootstrap resampling.

### 4.2 Exact Euclidean Orthogonal Distance Regression for Conics and Polygons
Algebraic conic fits (e.g., minimizing $A x^2 + B xy + C y^2 + D x + E y + F$) introduce severe curvature bias on partial arcs. All terminal-boundary models in `step4_endface_and_conic_models.py` are therefore fitted by minimizing the **exact Euclidean orthogonal distance** $d_{\perp, i}$ from each point $(x_i, y_i)$ to the model curve via Levenberg–Marquardt / Trust-Region Reflective optimization (`scipy.optimize.least_squares`), where at each solver evaluation the foot-point parameter $\psi_i$ on the curve is found to machine precision via 8 steps of Newton–Raphson iteration:
- **M1 (Circle, $k=3$)**: $(x_c, y_c, R)$.
- **M2 (Bisector-Constrained Ellipse, $k=4$)**: $(x_c, y_c, a, b)$ with minor axis fixed to the longitudinal bisector $\theta_{\text{bis}} = 47.552^\circ$ (required for an unburied right circular cone or cylinder).
- **M3 (Dorsal-Constrained Ellipse, $k=4$)**: $(x_c, y_c, a, b)$ with minor axis fixed to the exposed dorsal limb $\theta_{\text{dorsal}} = 29.878^\circ$ (required for a right circular cylinder partially buried along its ventral flank).
- **M4 (Free General Ellipse, $k=5$)**: $(x_c, y_c, a, b, \phi_{\text{minor}})$ initialized across a multi-start angular grid.
- **M5 (Parabolic Arch, $k=4$)**: $(x_v, y_v, c_2, \phi)$ where $u = c_2 v^2$ in rotated vertex coordinates.
- **M6a / M6b (Polygonal Angular Facet Rim, $k=4$ for 2 segments, $k=6$ for 3 segments)**: Piecewise-linear polyline minimizing exact point-to-segment Euclidean distance.

### 4.3 Information-Theoretic Model Selection
Models applied to the same boundary dataset ($N$ points) are compared using weighted chi-squared $\chi^2 = \sum_{i=1}^N (d_{\perp, i} / \sigma_{\text{eff}, i})^2$, reduced chi-squared $\chi^2_\nu = \chi^2 / (N - k)$, Akaike Information Criterion with finite-sample correction ($\text{AIC}_c$), and Bayesian Information Criterion ($\text{BIC}$):
$$\text{AIC}_c = \chi^2 + 2k + \frac{2k(k+1)}{N - k - 1}, \quad \text{BIC} = \chi^2 + k \ln N$$

## 5. Assumptions and Explicit Scope Boundaries

1. **Monocular Scale Ambiguity**: Because `3556MR1025170721700585C00` is a single-camera observation without overlapping stereo coverage of the target object, absolute distance $D$ along the camera ray cannot be triangulated from disparity. Metric dimensions are therefore parameterized continuously as a function of slant range $D$, bounded by the Mastcam autofocus sweep (`MINIMUM_FOCUS_DISTANCE = 3.6 m`, `BEST_FOCUS_DISTANCE = 3.957 m`, `MAXIMUM_FOCUS_DISTANCE = 4.4 m`) and horizontal ground-plane intersections ($3.55\text{ m}$ in `ROVER_NAV_FRAME`, $5.18\text{ m}$ in `SITE_FRAME`). Dimensionless angular and pixel ratios are strictly invariant to $D$.
2. **Distinction Between Physical Rim and Cast Shadow**: We do not assume *a priori* that the dark region at the lower-right of the object is an open cavity or a solid end-face. Instead, we partition the boundary by physical contrast and solar shadow ray-tracing ($\theta_{\text{shadow}} = +63.43^\circ$) and evaluate models across four nested angular subsets (`S1`: $140^\circ$, `S2`: $160^\circ$, `S3`: $250^\circ$, `S4`: $360^\circ$).

## 6. Targeted Correction for Upper-Right Pebble Outlier and Rear Terminal Contour Extraction (`step8_pebble_correction_and_rear_terminus.py`)

### 6.1 Single Outlying Pebble Point Identification on the Front Rim
Direct inspection of the 36-point radial front-rim profile (`theta` $\in [+150^\circ, +180^\circ] \cup [-180^\circ, -35^\circ]$ from $(1067.5, 985.0)$ in `DRXX`) shows that 35 of the 36 subpixel rim points follow the dark inner opening boundary smoothly, whereas **only one single far outlying point** — at $\theta = -45.0^\circ$ ($x = 1083.34, y = 969.16$, $r_{\text{erf}} = 22.40\text{ px}$) — jumps $+2.4\text{ px}$ outward across the narrow shadow gap onto the separate adjacent upper-right pebble ($x \in [1072, 1084], y \in [961, 970]$). The immediately adjacent rim stations at $\theta = -50.0^\circ$ ($1080.51, 969.50$, $r = 19.86\text{ px}$) and $\theta = -40.0^\circ$ ($1083.09, 971.92$, $r = 20.34\text{ px}$) lie on the target's rim. In `step8_pebble_correction_and_rear_terminus.py`, we exclude only this single outlying pebble point ($\theta = -45.0^\circ$), retaining all 35 clean front-rim points (as well as all 26 dorsal longitudinal stations and 22 clean ventral stations) and preserving the uncorrected 36-point fit for direct comparison.

### 6.2 Rear Terminal Contour Extraction Along the Blue-to-Green/Yellow Radiometric Boundary
At the upper-left (rear) termination of the sunlit body (`x` $\in [1027, 1046]$, `y` $\in [944, 959]$), the background soil exhibits spectral luminance $I \le 0.080\text{ W m}^{-2}\text{ sr}^{-1}\text{ nm}^{-1}$ (darker blue in `viridis`), whereas the sunlit object exhibits $I \ge 0.088\text{ W m}^{-2}\text{ sr}^{-1}\text{ nm}^{-1}$ (lighter greens and yellows in `viridis`). To trace this boundary without clipping the protruding left nose at $(1028.0, 950.0)$ or jumping into the dark background pits at $y = 944\text{–}945$ (and suppressing the single detached 1-pixel background speckle at $(1040, 945)$ separated from the body by $(1040, 946)$ at $I = 0.081$), we cast 36 radial profiles outward from the rear-cap interior center $(1035.5, 951.2)$ across $\theta \in [-210^\circ, -35^\circ]$ in $5^\circ$ increments ($r \in [2.0, 11.5]\text{ px}$). Along each ray, we locate the innermost outward crossing of the midpoint threshold $I_{\text{iso}} = 0.0860\text{ W m}^{-2}\text{ sr}^{-1}\text{ nm}^{-1}$ and fit a local 1D error-function (`erf`) transition within $\pm 1.5\text{ px}$ of that crossing. We then apply identical orthogonal distance regression (`ODR`) conic and polygonal models (`M1`–`M6b`) and homothetic front-face shape translation/scaling tests to compare the curvature of the rear terminus directly against the clean front face.

## 7. Joint Geometric Modeling & Collinear Axis Resolution (`step9_joint_geometric_model.py`)

To eliminate uncoupled parameter freedom and test whether a single continuous geometric cylinder accounts simultaneously for all visible aspects of the object, `step9_joint_geometric_model.py` performs joint optimization across $N = 85$ measured boundary points:
1. **Front Rim Points ($N = 35$)**: Clean overhanging rim arc excluding the single pebble-contaminated outlier at $\theta = -45^\circ$.
2. **Exposed Rear Arc Points ($N = 24$)**: Clean rear terminal contour ($i = 8\dots 31$, $\theta \in [-170^\circ, -55^\circ]$).
3. **Upper Exterior Stations ($N = 26$)**: Subpixel points along the straight upper longitudinal boundary where the sunlit body meets dark background regolith.

### 7.1 Mathematical Formulations of the Joint Models
- **Model 1: Strict Right Circular Cylinder (6 DOF)**:
  - Free parameters: $\mathbf{p}_1 = (x_{c,f}, y_{c,f}, a, b, L_{\text{proj}}, \theta_{\text{axis}})$.
  - Constraints:
    - Coaxial projected centers: $\mathbf{X}_{\text{rear}} = \mathbf{X}_{\text{front}} - L_{\text{proj}} (\cos\theta_{\text{axis}}, \sin\theta_{\text{axis}})^T$.
    - Identical cross-section: $a_{\text{rear}} = a_{\text{front}} = a$, $b_{\text{rear}} = b_{\text{front}} = b$, and minor axis orientation $\phi_{\text{minor}} = \theta_{\text{axis}}$.
    - Strict silhouette tangency: The upper straight boundary is strictly tangent to the outer envelope of the front and rear cross-sections at transverse offset $r_{\text{dor}} = a$, with zero uncoupled offset ($\Delta r = 0$).
  - Residuals: Exact orthogonal distance from each of the 85 points to its corresponding geometric feature (front ellipse, rear ellipse, or upper tangent line).
- **Model 2: Right Circular Cylinder with Free Dorsal Offset (7 DOF)**:
  - Free parameters: $\mathbf{p}_2 = (x_{c,f}, y_{c,f}, a, b, L_{\text{proj}}, \theta_{\text{axis}}, r_{\text{dor}})$.
  - Allows an uncoupled transverse offset $r_{\text{dor}}$ between the cylinder axis and the measured upper edge to test whether an internal reflection or crest line is being measured.
- **Model 3: Unweighted Cylinder-Compatible Fit (7 Effective Parameters)**:
  - Scans $\theta_{\text{axis}} \in [28.0^\circ, 35.0^\circ]$ to find the unweighted minimum total RMS residual across all 85 points with equal station weighting.
- **Model 4: Tapered Conical Frustum with Coupled Silhouette Edge (8 DOF)**:
  - Free parameters: $\mathbf{p}_4 = (x_{c,f}, y_{c,f}, a, b, L_{\text{proj}}, \theta_{\text{axis}}, s, r_{\text{dor}})$, where $s = a_{\text{rear}}/a_{\text{front}}$ is the taper scale ratio.
  - The upper silhouette edge angle is coupled to the body axis via the taper angle: $\theta_{\text{dor}} = \theta_{\text{axis}} - \arctan((1-s)a/L_{\text{proj}})$.

### 7.2 Profile Likelihood Analysis of the Taper Scale Ratio $s$
To test whether the data mandate a tapered cone ($s < 1$) or are consistent with a constant-diameter cylinder ($s = 1$), the scale ratio $s$ is scanned over $s \in [0.80, 1.20]$. At each fixed $s$, the rear arc is fitted by finding the optimal center $(x_{c,r}, y_{c,r})$. The resulting profile likelihood $\Delta\text{RMS}(s)$ quantifies the degree to which arc curvature constrains tapering:
$$\Delta\text{RMS}(s) = \text{RMS}_{\text{rear}}(s) - \text{RMS}_{\text{rear}}(s_{\text{opt}})$$
Over a single-quadrant arc ($N = 24$, span $\approx 90^\circ$), changes in $s$ are almost perfectly degenerate with a subpixel shift of the center $(x_{c,r}, y_{c,r})$ along the chord normal, yielding $\Delta\text{RMS} < 0.025\text{ px}$ across the entire range $s \in [0.85, 1.15]$.

## 8. Comparative Smoothness, Straightness, and Photometric Shading Metrics

To quantitatively benchmark the target object against surrounding natural Martian geology in the same image frame:
1. **Edge Straightness & Sagitta Analysis**:
   - For an extracted boundary spanning longitudinal length $L$, the best-fit line is found via orthogonal distance regression.
   - Orthogonal RMS residual $\sigma_\perp = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum v_i^2}$ measures high-frequency wobble/roughness.
   - Maximum sagitta deviation $\delta_{\text{sag}} = |c_2| (L/2)^2$ from quadratic regression $v(u) = c_0 + c_1 u + c_2 u^2$ measures large-scale curvature.
   - Evaluated comparatively against the 3 nearest well-defined rock edges in the immediate vicinity ($x \in [1010, 1120], y \in [920, 1020]$).
2. **Surface Texture via Discrete Laplacian Variance**:
   - On normalized radiance patches, high-frequency texture is quantified by the variance of the discrete Laplacian operator:
     $$\sigma_{\text{Lap}}^2 = \operatorname{Var}\left(\nabla^2 I\right)$$
   - Smooth surfaces (e.g. machined metal or uniform coating) yield low variance, whereas natural pitted, vesicular, or fractured sedimentary rocks exhibit significantly elevated variance.
3. **Transverse Photometric Shading (Cylindrical vs. Planar Facet)**:
   - Evaluates whether cross-sectional luminance $I(v)$ follows a quadratic convex roll-off characteristic of a round cylindrical body:
     $$I(v) = c_0 + c_1 v + c_2 v^2$$
   - The statistical significance of curvature $z = |c_2| / \sigma_{c_2}$ evaluates the rejection of the flat-faceted rock hypothesis ($c_2 = 0$).
