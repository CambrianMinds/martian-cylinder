Abstract
A centimeter-scale object in Curiosity Mastcam-100 imagery acquired on sol 3556 exhibits two curved termi-
nations and a straight upper exterior boundary. Physically directed subpixel localization yields 35 near-rim,
24 far-termination, and 26 upper-boundary stations. A six-parameter common-section, common-axis model
with exact upper tangency fits all 85 observations at 0.3788 px RMS. More importantly, a front/dorsal-
derived ellipse with frozen size, shape, and orientation predicts rear points beyond those used for trans-
lation: six anchors predict the other 18 stations at 0.2084 px RMS, and guarded contiguous rear holdouts
yield 0.2429 px. Guarded spatial holdouts of the strict model yield 0.7233 px across all three features.
Withholding the entire dorsal boundary is reported separately as a cross-feature sensitivity test. An earlier
tapered formulation achieves a lower numerical residual but violates its own ellipse–line tangency condition
and is rejected as a physical frustum. A completed comparison of 28 Curiosity images, including 18 uni-
formly measured whole frames, demonstrates comparable straight segments in geological scenes but does
not establish the frequency of the full measured conjunction. The configuration exhibits manufacturing-like
geometric regularity supported by predictive tests; composition and provenance are not determined.
Keywords: Mars; Gale crater; Curiosity; Mastcam-100; cylinder; subpixel metrology; orthogonal regression; image
geometry; occlusion; concretion; technosignature assessment.
1. Introduction
A photograph can establish the projected geometry of an object even when its composition, depth, and origin
remain unknown. For the Curiosity Mastcam-100 observation considered here, the central question is unusually
specific: can the distinct curved boundary at the foreground end, the exposed curvature at the far end, and the
upper longitudinal boundary be represented by one common cylindrical geometry? The central evidentiary
question is whether that geometry predicts observed boundary points that did not participate in its registration
or fit. Determining physical origin is beyond the present scope.

The target occurs in the image identified by 3556MR1025170721700585C00, acquired on sol 3556 (7 August
2022). The Mastcam-100 instrument is a fixed-focal-length, narrow-field camera with an instantaneous field
of view near 74 𝜇rad per image pixel, making subpixel analysis appropriate for centimeter-scale objects at
several meters’ range [3, 4]. The target is not a full, free-standing object on a uniform background: its lower
outline merges with the regolith, and a dark region adjacent to the near end contains a cast-shadow contribution.
Consequently, not every visible intensity transition belongs to the solid’s perimeter.
We define material boundaries independently of their suitability for any candidate shape. The upper edge is
selected at the bright-body/dark-regolith interface; the lower visible contact is classified as possible foreground
soil occlusion rather than automatically being treated as a second silhouette generator. Both terminal curves
are registered with explicit parameter constraints. The measured feature families are then tested jointly against
constant-section and tapered geometries. The image data and executable analyses are retained in the research
repository [1].
This paper reports measurements, predictive validation, uncertainty structure, and the completed geological
comparison. To our knowledge, this is the first publicly documented, reproducible quantitative metrology study
of this particular feature. Earlier recognition and visualization are acknowledged: Ward credits Rami Bar Ilan
with discovery, and MILTOID presented Bayer decoding and a visual 3D cylinder overlay in October 2025
[9, 10]. Those preliminary checks precede the numerical metrology and predictive tests reported here. The
original study appears in the repository history on 24 October 2026 and in the Zenodo deposit published 30
October; independent reconstruction was added on 11 November, followed by the 13 November predictive
validation [11].

2.2 Four distinguishable visual regions
The segmentation distinguishes (i) the sunlit longitudinal surface, (ii) its upper background-facing edge, (iii)
the foreground curved termination separating illuminated material from the dark recess, and (iv) the lower-right
dark patch, whose distal boundary partly follows cast shadow on soil rather than the object’s cross-section. The
far upper-left termination has its own observable material/background boundary, while its lower part is obscured
by regolith.
This classification is causally important. An elliptical fit to a shadow boundary measures illumination ge-
ometry and ground topography, not necessarily the perimeter of the object that cast the shadow. Likewise, a
line running through the brightest surface is not necessarily the outer silhouette, even if its projected slope is
nearly constant.
The analysis reports a regolith-noise estimate 𝜎𝐼= 7.782 × 10−3 in its radiometric units, a sunlit-object
mean near 9.848 × 10−2, and a dark-core mean near 1.358 × 10−2, with an edge signal-to-noise estimate of
10.91 for its selected contrast definition. These measurements characterize signal separation without assuming
anything about the object’s material.

4.2 Exterior-boundary validation
The upper boundary is defined by the large, spatially coherent radiometric transition from background regolith
into the illuminated body. At each of 26 stations, the extractor records transition contrast, gradient direction,
fitted transition position, and fit uncertainty. All 26 stations satisfy the expected polarity diagnostic. Their best-
fit line is at 28.895∘, with 0.2422 px orthogonal RMS over approximately 36.4 px of the visible exterior. The
edge-coordinate arrays and diagnostic fields are retained in step3_features.json. This feature definition is
independent of the subsequent cylinder or tapered-model fits.
4.3 Near rim: separation from the adjacent pebble
The near curved termination is sampled at five-degree radial intervals. Of 36 near-rim stations, precisely one
at 𝜃= −45∘lies across a narrow shadow gap on the adjacent upper-right pebble. Its extracted position is
approximately (1083.34, 969.16), radius 22.40 px, compared with neighboring radii of 19.86 and 20.34 px.
The near-rim dataset therefore comprises the 35 stations that lie on the continuous observed termination, with
the neighboring pebble excluded by spatial and contrast classification.

4.4 Far termination: separating cap, soil, and longitudinal transition
The rear region is sampled from an interior seed at (1035.5, 951.2), with radial angles spanning −210∘to −35∘.
The extractor selects the first outward crossing of a radiometric threshold 𝐼iso = 0.0860 before fitting the local

treated as the terminating curve. This prevents a soil-occlusion line from masquerading as a noncircular end.

The 6-parameter model’s residuals should be understood in relation to its geometric obligations, not only
relative to its competitors. It simultaneously puts the rear and near curves on one axis, fixes their common
section size, and makes the measured upper exterior edge tangent to that section. Its small, distributed residuals
therefore constitute positive evidence for cylindrical consistency. M4’s smaller numerical residual does not
restore its failed physical tangency constraint.
6.2 Near- and far-termination agreement
Independently of the joint fit, the translated unchanged near ellipse matches the far arc at 0.18629 px RMS. The
far curve covers only part of the termination; it has no requirement to expose its complete lower perimeter. This
registration is therefore evidence that the visible part of the far terminus can carry the same local curvature as
the near rim, not an observation of two complete closed end faces.
The test is stronger than a statement that each curved boundary can be approximated by an unrelated ellipse:
shape, orientation, and scale are held common. It is weaker than a stereo reconstruction of both terminal planes.
The distinction follows directly from the degrees of freedom and the observation geometry.
6.3 Predictive validation beyond registration points
The new tests preserve the existing coordinates and exclusions. Their protocol was written before these vali-
dation runs; the tests are retrospective coordinate-level checks, not a blind repeat of boundary selection. Two
front-shape references must be distinguished. The freely oriented ellipse above gives 0.18629 px rear RMS. The
predictive tests instead freeze the front ellipse constrained to the measured dorsal orientation: 𝑎= 20.02897 px,
𝑏= 16.84577 px, 𝜃= 28.89475∘, and scale 𝑠= 1. Fitting only its rear translation reproduces 0.1854 px RMS.
Neither rear size nor orientation is adjusted.

33.185∘, and 28.895∘, respectively. The near ellipse is observed along an incomplete curve, allowing its angle to

is approximately 4.23∘. A transverse shift of 1 px over a 40.6 px center baseline changes its bearing by ap-
proximately 1.41∘. These sensitivities put the several-degree differences in context. Joint optimization chooses
30.315∘for the strict cylinder, and achieves subpixel residuals on each point family.
6.5 Occlusion versus intrinsic taper
The upper exterior boundary and lower visible diagonal need not represent two opposite silhouette generators.
Their measured apparent angular convergence is approximately 35∘, whereas perspective convergence between
parallel generators at the measured camera scale is below 0.4∘for the considered axis inclinations. This excludes
the interpretation of both visible lines as the unoccluded opposite sides of a parallel-sided body, but does not
exclude a partially buried cylinder.
In the partially buried geometry, the upper transition is a material silhouette, while the lower diagonal is
the contact between the body and foreground regolith. The latter can cut obliquely across a cylindrical surface
as burial depth changes along its length. The two image lines therefore encode different physical boundaries,
and their angular separation is not an estimator of cylinder taper without first establishing that both are true
generators.
6.6 Taper: two different statistical questions
The rear-arc comparison and the coupled cone fitting answer distinct questions. A standalone fit translates an
otherwise fixed near-ellipse shape and optionally scales it by 𝑠, using only 24 exposed rear points. Its minimum
occurs at 𝑠≈0.93 with 0.18434 px RMS, while the exact equal-size case 𝑠= 1 yields 0.18629 px, a difference
of 0.00195 px. Across 𝑠= 0.85–1.15, the variations in raw residual remain small in the context of the image
sampling. The conditional profile-likelihood interval recorded by the pipeline is [0.87, 0.99]; it does not literally
include 𝑠= 1 under that specific weighting model. Raw-fit proximity and formal confidence limits are different
statements.
The coupled eight-parameter tapered formulation fits all 85 points at once. Its best-fit 𝑠= 0.6187, with a
nominal profile interval [0.61, 0.63], is associated with 𝜃≈39.33∘and 𝛼≈9.46∘. Shrinking the far section
allows its proposed silhouette line to rotate toward the observed upper-edge angle, lowering RMS to 0.2549 px.

8. Texture, Straightness, and Illumination as Independent Constraints
The selected exterior edge has 0.2422 px orthogonal line RMS over a sampled span of approximately 36.4 px.
At the focus-distance reference scale that corresponds to an image-plane residual length of about 71 𝜇m. This
is a projected measurement residual, not profilometric roughness of a three-dimensional surface at micrometer
resolution. A quadratic curvature estimate for the same edge gives a maximum sagitta of 0.280 px.
The completed comparison uses 28 retrieved Curiosity images, including 18 whole Mastcam frames mea-
sured uniformly and ten captioned geological/context images. A common contour method applies 2 × 2 area
averaging, brightness-level contours, and 26 equally spaced samples over a 36.4-native-pixel span. Its target
window is selected by proximity to the existing dorsal stations, not minimum RMS. It yields 0.303 px RMS, a
separate measurement from the full-resolution erf result of 0.2422 px.
Among 226,426 control windows, 835 (0.369%) are at least as straight; qualifying segments occur in 17 of
18 frames. Straight segments therefore occur in geological scenes. These overlapping photometric windows are
correlated and may follow material, fracture, shadow, or shading boundaries; they are not counts of independent
rocks. Their fraction is not an origin probability. Smoothing and span change the conditional fraction, so the
full sensitivity tables are retained in the supplementary comparison report [12].
The conjunction of terminal curvature, transferred geometry, and longitudinal constraint remains the relevant
object-level result. The comparison did not estimate the frequency of that complete conjunction. Its automatic
rim contour differs from the recorded rim stations by 2.88 pixels on average, preventing a comparable rim rank-
ing; appearance-search scores likewise are not physical-model likelihoods. No full counterpart of the target’s
85-station reconstruction was demonstrated by that study. The detailed image inventory, comparison atlas,
preprocessing sensitivity, and appearance retrievals remain supplementary material.
Three locally selected natural rock boundaries in the same image provide secondary context. Their reported
line RMS values are 0.971, 1.724, and 1.953 px, respectively, compared with 0.242 px for the target (table 6).
The target edge is therefore approximately four to eight times straighter in this specific local comparison. This
describes the three selected local boundaries; the completed comparison above supplies the broader measured

9. Geometric Implications and Scope
The evidence is cumulative. The front rim constrains the section; the exposed rear curve tests transferred
curvature; and the measured dorsal boundary constrains orientation and exterior tangency. The six-parameter
model imposes these relations jointly. Sparse registration and guarded holdouts add evidence beyond a fitted
overlay: untrained rear stations continue to follow the frozen shape with approximately 0.2–0.24 px RMS. A
straight sedimentary edge alone is not the identifying result.
Constant-section engineered elements possess the geometric relations tested here. The measured config-
uration therefore exhibits manufacturing-like geometric regularity, with predictive support rather than visual
resemblance alone. The completed geological comparison has not demonstrated a natural analogue reproducing
the measured conjunction. Neither naming abrasion, fracture, concretion, or sediment fluidization nor pointing
to a straight edge supplies a quantitative explanation of this configuration. Those processes remain documented
geological context [5, 6, 7, 8, 13]; this study does not require eliminating every conceivable natural process.
Manufacture is a morphologically motivated hypothesis, while determining origin is beyond the present scope.
9.1 Measurement and interpretation limits
The validation is retrospective and uses selected coordinates from one acquisition. Neighboring samples share
optical and interpolation information, even with coordinate-level guard bands. The nominal reduced chi-square
of 33.59 signals an inadequate error/model description; correlation, underestimated localization errors, and
model discrepancy are possible contributors, not separately measured explanations. No calibrated origin odds,
coverage-calibrated joint confidence interval, or model ranking follows from those nominal weights. Model-
specific Birge rescaling is not a common likelihood or a fitted spatial covariance.
The partial terminal arcs constrain visible boundaries rather than both complete hidden end faces. The shared
cylindrical model is a constrained image-plane representation, not a unique three-dimensional reconstruction.
Focus-based range and receiving-plane assumptions are conditional. Pixel-to-micrometer conversions quantify
projected residual distances at an assumed range, not independently measured machining tolerance or surface
roughness. The present photometry (𝑧= 0.95) does not independently establish curved shading.
The comparison study measures photometric features, not a population frequency of the full geometry. Its
appearance and straightness rankings cannot be converted into probabilities of natural or manufactured origin.
Composition, provenance, and a unique concealed shape remain undetermined. Additional observations could
address them, but they are not prerequisites for reporting the measured predictive consistency.

11. Conclusion
Physically directed subpixel measurements yield three mutually constraining features: a 35-point near rim, a
24-point exposed far termination, and a 26-point dorsal boundary. Their strict common-axis, common-section,
tangent-boundary model fits all 85 observations at 0.3788 px RMS. More decisively, the frozen front/dorsal-
derived shape predicts eighteen rear stations after six-anchor registration at 0.2084 px RMS, and guarded rear
holdouts at 0.2429 px. Guarded spatial prediction across all three feature families yields 0.7233 px. The tested
M4 formulation fails its own physical tangency condition.
The measured configuration exhibits manufacturing-like geometric regularity, supported by frozen-shape
transfer and heldout prediction. The completed geological comparison has not demonstrated a natural analogue
reproducing the measured conjunction. These findings establish a quantitative morphological anomaly and
motivate manufacture as a hypothesis; they do not determine composition or provenance.

B. Projection and Identifiability Details
For an ideal projected circular end plane with normal aligned to the three-dimensional cylinder axis, the end
appears elliptical under oblique viewing. In an orthographic approximation its axis ratio satisfies 𝑏/𝑎≈| cos 𝛾|,

C. Sensitivity, Resolution, and Error Interpretation
The spatial residual 0.3788 px is not the per-point localization uncertainty, the actual physical surface error of
the object, or the probability that a cylinder is true. It is the raw root-mean-square distance of selected image
observations from one defined six-parameter model. At the reference image scale from eq. (7), it corresponds
to about 0.111 mm in the projected image plane.
The workflow samples several overlapping descriptions of the same optical signal: error-function transition
centers, parabolically interpolated gradient extrema, three radiance bands, linearized camera products, and
trimmed subsets. Reassuring agreement across these comparisons demonstrates stability to those processing
choices. Because neighboring stations and demosaiced bands are not fully independent, the effective number
of independent observations for formal significance testing may be less than the literal station count. The six-
parameter fit remains a reproducible geometric description regardless of how a future error covariance model
is refined.
