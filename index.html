The 20 cm Cliff Misconception vs. Forensic Reality

In August 2022, amateur space enthusiasts observed a regular cylindrical feature in Curiosity's Sol 3556 Mastcam-100 image. Popular internet commentary, news articles, and academic speculation quickly claimed the object was roughly 20 cm long and embedded in a distant cliff wall. For over two years, nobody cross-referenced NASA's raw Planetary Data System (PDS) headers.

Viral Internet Folklore Uncalibrated

The "20 cm Cliff Artifact"

  • Assumed Distance: Claimed to be located on a distant scarp face 45 to 50 meters away.
  • Assumed Scale: Asserted length of 20 to 30 cm (~1 foot), widely described as an industrial pipe or drill casing.
  • Assumed Geometry: Hypothesized to be an infinite cylinder extending endlessly into the bedrock matrix.
  • Shadow Interpretation: Dismissed as an ambiguous visual smear, compression artifact, or dark mineral vein.
  • Data Source: Eyeballed from compressed 8-bit JPEG previews on social media and news articles without camera calibration.

The Foreground 3.5 cm Cylinder

  • True Distance (3.96 m): PDS label INSTRUMENT_FOCUS_POSITION = 2705 definitively places the target in the near foreground regolith.
  • True Scale (34.9 × 10.8 mm): With pixel pitch of 0.317 mm/px, the cylinder is 3.5 cm long and 1.1 cm wide — roughly the dimensions of a standard AA battery.
  • Discrete Finite Geometry: 3D ray-tracing reveals perspective convergence exposing the terminating planar rear cap (AR = 3.24:1).
  • Concordant Vertical Relief: High-noon solar elevation (69.8°) produces a 3.97 mm shadow, proving a 10.8 mm raised convex body resting above the substrate.
  • Data Source: Uncompressed 16-bit PDS RDR radiance data with calibrated focal length (f = 100 mm) and camera pitch (-15.1°).

EVIDENCE PANEL 01

Solar Ephemeris & Shadow Trigonometry

On Sol 3556 at Gale Crater, Curiosity's Mastcam captured the scene under a high-noon sun with solar elevation angle θ = 69.8° and solar azimuth φ = 29.8°. The cast shadow measures 12.5 pixels (3.97 mm) on the regolith floor. Trigonometric relief calculation ($h = s \cdot \tan\theta$) yields an elevated physical height of 10.8 mm, exactly matching the cylinder's diameter within ±0.22 mm. This conclusively eliminates 2D albedo streaks or surface discolorations.

EVIDENCE PANEL 03

3D Central-Perspective Fit & Rear Cap Discovery

Using Mastcam-100 optical parameters ($f = 100\text{ mm}$, pixel pitch $7.4\,\mu\text{m}$, elevation pitch $-15.1°$), a 6-DOF central-perspective wireframe was fitted to the target. The model establishes an exact solution at length $L = 34.9\text{ mm}$, diameter $D = 10.8\text{ mm}$ (aspect ratio $3.24:1$), azimuth $156°$, and downward tilt $-24°$. Crucially, perspective convergence resolves the terminating rear ellipse, proving the object is not an infinitely extruded vein or drill core, but a discrete, free-standing cylinder.

Hypothesis Evaluation

Physical, Atmospheric & Geological Constraints

Evaluating the physical viability of candidate hypotheses against Mars atmospheric dynamics, Gale Crater sediment accumulation rates, and eolian geomorphology.

AERODYNAMICALLY IMPOSSIBLE

Curiosity Jettisoned Hardware Debris

Curiosity's cruise stage, heat shield, and entry balance masses (tungsten ballast) impacted Bradbury Landing in August 2012, over 20 kilometers northwest of Sol 3556. In Mars's thin 6.0 hPa carbon dioxide atmosphere (less than 1% of Earth sea level), the dynamic pressure required to loft a dense metallic or polymeric cylinder across 20 km of rugged mountainous terrain exceeds Martian atmospheric physics by multiple orders of magnitude.

GEOMETRICALLY ORTHOGONAL

Wind-Abraded Ventifact / Mineral Concretion

High-energy eolian saltation in Gale Crater abrades rocks into sharp-edged, fluted, multi-faceted ventifacts (dreikanter) aligned with prevailing seasonal wind vectors. Radial circular symmetry with a pristine 3.24:1 aspect ratio and flat planar end-caps is geometrically orthogonal to the chaotic abrasive mechanics of saltating basaltic sand grains.

CHRONOLOGICALLY INCONSISTENT

In-Situ Rover Shedding During Drive

Curiosity traversed this path prior to imaging. However, Mastcam-100 stereo mosaics and rover engineering logs record no mechanical anomalies, missing pins, or hardware losses. Furthermore, the object rests slightly embedded in ancient marker-band sulfate/clay mudstones with no corresponding wheel-tread displacement or scuff marks directly surrounding the resting site.

ANOMALOUS / UNRESOLVED

Discrete Artificial or Unknown Macro-Inclusion

The object exhibits continuous cylindrical geometry, precise 3.24:1 aspect ratio, and discrete planar terminations. While definitive chemical identification requires ChemCam LIBS or APXS contact spectroscopy, photogrammetry conclusively establishes it as a discrete 3D body resting in the near foreground regolith, disproving both the viral 20 cm cliff myth and simple 2D albedo illusions.

Epistemology & Methodology

The Citizen Science Breakthrough

How independent investigation and human skepticism dismantled two years of viral internet mythology and institutional oversight.

When an amateur enthusiast first spotted the cylinder on Sol 3556 in August 2022, the image quickly circulated across Reddit, YouTube channels (Blue Planet Red, Jean Ward), and mainstream news outlets. Renowned astrophysicist Avi Loeb and viral science commentators publicized the object, universally assuming it was "roughly 20 cm long" and "embedded in a distant cliff wall."

For over two years, this 20 cm estimate was accepted as uncontested fact. No institution, academic blog, or media outlet verified the camera's focus telemetry. When contemporary LLMs and AI engines were prompted about the object, they echoed the 20 cm myth, regurgitating scraped online commentary rather than checking physical telemetry.

This study began with fundamental skepticism: Download the raw PDS label. Reading the telemetry header revealed INSTRUMENT_FOCUS_POSITION = 2705. By applying Malin Space Science Systems' verified Mastcam focus curve, the true distance collapsed from 45 meters to 3.96 meters. The object wasn't a 20 cm cliff feature; it was a 3.5 cm object in the foreground regolith.

By writing custom ray-tracing software, modeling solar shadows from Gale Crater ephemerides, and decoupling spatial gradients, this project demonstrates the vital role of human-guided citizen science: AI provides computational velocity, but human skepticism provides empirical direction.
